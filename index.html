Foundations

Six Best Practices

1 Before the tactics

  • Don't write for the algorithm. Best practices can lift reach and ignoring them can cost reach, but they decide how you post, not what you post.
  • Good content can become great content. Bad content stays bad content. No amount of timing fixes a post nobody wanted to read.
  • Write for your target audience, not for reach. People can tell when a post exists to farm engagement.

2 The six practices

Post when you can block off 30 minutes

You need to be active right after posting, so pick a time you can actually protect.

Engage with 3+ posts in your feed after you post

Immediately after publishing. It signals you're active on the platform, not just broadcasting.

Don't edit inside the first 10 minutes

Edits during the initial distribution window are widely reported to suppress reach. Proof before you hit post.

Keep links out of the post body

Put the link in the first comment or in a follow-up. Outbound links pull people off the platform and reach drops.

Use 3 to 5 relevant hashtags

Enough to be categorized, not so many it reads as spam.

Reply to every comment in the first hour

The single highest-leverage habit on this list. Replies count as engagement and pull the post back into circulation.

The playbook these came from was written when the platform behaved a certain way. The mechanics that don't change: post consistently, be present when you post, reply to everyone, and write for a specific person. The mechanics that do change: hashtag counts, link penalties, edit windows.

Treat the six above as defaults to test, not laws. Track your own numbers for a month and keep what holds up.

Key

Missing one of these doesn't make the post a failure. They are defaults, not requirements.

Foundations

Daily Posting Routine

1 The five steps

Log in 15 minutes before you post

There's work to do before the post goes out.

Like and comment on 5 posts

Scroll, find something you actually like, and leave a real comment. One to two minutes per post — don't overthink it.

Send 5 connection requests

Search the job title of your ideal customer, scroll, and send five requests to people who look interesting.

Post

If you're using a scheduler, this already happened.

Reply to every comment in the first hour

Early on your comment count will be low. That's normal — it builds with frequency. Consider DMing everyone who engages to say thanks and ask what landed for them.

2 What this costs you

  • About 45 minutes a day once the routine is built, including writing.
  • Five requests a day is 100+ a month. At a 50% accept rate that's 600 ideal-customer connections a year.
  • The DM follow-up is where the business happens. The post starts the conversation; the DM continues it.
Foundations

Writing Rules

1 Structure

The 1-3-1 structure
One sentence.

One sentence. One sentence. One sentence.

One sentence.
  • Line 1 gives an easy start. Short, no ramp-up, no throat-clearing.
  • The middle three deliver the value. This is where the substance goes.
  • The last line cuts it back. One line to land it.
  • White space is doing work. Blocks of text get skipped on mobile.

2 Word choice

  • Write dumber. Simpler language reaches more people and takes less energy to read. Aim at a seven-year-old's vocabulary.
  • Cut every word that isn't adding anything. If removing it changes nothing, remove it.
  • Short sentences. One or three ideas, not five. Bullets instead of commas — if a sentence needs two commas, start again.
  • Pick a simple word over a clever word. Every time.

3 Hooks and headlines

  • Short hooks outperform long ones. Six words or fewer is the pattern in most top posts; three is the sweet spot.
  • Readers skip words. Write assuming half your sentence never gets read.
  • Be specific. '7 blueberry muffins' beats 'some muffins.'
  • Don't hedge. 'This is the best' beats 'this might be a good.'
Foundations

Content Mix

1 Four formats to rotate

Long, deep, knowledge posts

Frameworks, processes, breakdowns. These build authority.

Quick actionable lists

Mistakes, tips, good vs bad. Easy to skim, easy to save.

Short opinion takes

Three lines. Attracts the people who think like you.

Relatable one-liners

Personality. No teaching. Keeps the feed unpredictable.

2 Why rotate

  • An audience that can predict your next post stops opening them.
  • Different formats reach different people — a one-liner reaches people who'd never open a 400-word breakdown.
  • It also keeps writing sustainable. Not every day has a framework in it.

3 Where the ideas come from

  • DM your target audience and ask what they're stuck on.
  • Listen on calls. The phrasing clients use is the phrasing that works in a hook.
  • Read your own comments. The question asked twice is a post.
  • Keep a running list. Pick three topics, write ten subtopics under each, and write one subtopic a week.

Tools & Tracking

1 A scheduler

  • Queue posts so publishing isn't a daily decision.
  • Save drafts and build a backlog on good writing days.
  • Browse a library of high-performing posts when you're stuck for an angle.
  • Keep a light CRM of the people you engage with regularly.
  • AI drafting is useful for finding an angle. Don't publish what it writes.

The source playbook recommends Taplio. Buffer, Hypefury and native LinkedIn scheduling all cover the basics if you only need the queue.

2 An analytics tool

  • Views, followers and engagement rate per post and over time.
  • A searchable library of everything you've published — this is the real value.
  • Your top performers become the source material for the next round of posts.

The source playbook recommends Shield. LinkedIn's own analytics cover most of this now; the gap is the historical post library.

3 What to actually track

MetricWhat it tells you
ImpressionsWhether the hook worked.
CommentsWhether the post gave people something to say.
Profile viewsWhether the post made anyone curious about you.
Inbound conversationsThe only number that pays.
Key

Repurpose the top performers. A post that worked once will work again in a different format three months later.

Hooks

How Hooks Work

1 What a hook has to do

  • Signal what they get. 'How to' promises education. Education reads as value.
  • Name a desired identity or a real problem. The second half of the hook is who this is for.
  • Be specific. Numbers, timeframes, and named outcomes beat general claims.
  • Create a small gap. Enough curiosity to click 'see more', not so much it's a riddle.

2 Numbers

  • Exact numbers read as true. '12.89 seconds' and '$5,750.86' outperform round numbers.
  • Absurd numbers read as emphasis. '99.9999% of proposals I see' isn't a statistic and nobody treats it as one — it stamps authority on an opinion.
  • Hyperbole is fine if the body earns it. '10x' is a promise the post has to pay off.

3 Words that do heavy lifting

Pull the reader in

  • Steal — free, slightly forbidden
  • Cheat — shortcut, secret
  • Simple / easy / effortless — accessible
  • Harsh truth — discomfort
  • Underrated — insider knowledge
  • Save yourself [X] months — shortcut with a cost avoided

Push them past

  • Vague nouns — "things", "stuff", "some"
  • Hedges — "might", "could be", "in my opinion"
  • Wind-ups — "I wanted to share a few thoughts on..."
  • Jargon nobody says out loud
  • Clever wordplay that hides the subject

4 How to write ten hooks fast

  • Take the post you want to write and write the hook ten different ways before choosing.
  • Take a hook that worked for someone else, rework it around your topic, and see if it holds up.
  • Keep a file of hooks you stopped scrolling for. That file is your swipe file.
